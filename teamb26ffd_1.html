The one idea

SENTINEL only reads the record.

You document in PointClickCare the way you always do.
SENTINEL reads what's there and shows what the record still needs — while there's still time to add it.

It never signs anything off. There is no "Complete" button in SENTINEL — because a tap can't prove a record is right, and it isn't SENTINEL's job to say so. A flag clears only when the documentation is in PCC and SENTINEL reads it.

The four states

Every item is in one of four states.

Flag

A correctable gap, still inside the window. There's time to document it.

Pass

The record supports the classification. Nothing needed.

Pending

Needs a clinician's judgment before anything is decided.

Exposure

The window closed — a late entry no longer counts as a timely correction.

A Flag is an opportunity, not a mark against anyone. The whole point is to catch it while it's still a Flag.

Two checkpoints in your shift

Two quiet checkpoints — right where you already work.

1 · Mid-shift

A passive count sits at the top of your Point of Care (POC) assignment screen in PCC — the resident list you already work from all shift. It updates as you document. No pop-ups, nobody singled out.

2 · Before handoff

About an hour before your shift ends — while you're still on the floor and there's time to act. It sits before report, on purpose — never at clock-out.

No separate app, no extra login, no signing in or out to see it — it lives at the top of the Point of Care (POC) assignment screen you already work from.

What SENTINEL will never do.

✕Not a performance evaluation. It looks at the record, not at you.
✕Not for personnel action. Ever. Full stop.
✕No separate app or login. It lives where you already work.
✕No "Complete" to tap. You document; it verifies.

7
The details that keep it fair

Built so it earns your trust.

Flag wrong? Dispute it — on the record.

A licensed nurse or MDS coordinator (not a CNA) can mark a flag “not correct” and record why. It’s a logged, accountable clinical judgment — kept visible to leadership, not erased — and it never certifies the record as complete.

Most-urgent first

When several items are open, the one with the soonest-closing window and most at stake shows first — you're never hunting.

The count is honest

SENTINEL re-checks the record about every 10 minutes, but it counts each open flag only once — the totals never inflate just because it keeps looking. What leadership sees is the real picture, not a number stacked against anyone.

"What changed" — every summary shows flags resolved in time, new flags, and anything that slipped past the window. That's the story, not just a list.

8
Quick answers

The questions people ask first.

Do I have to log in or install anything?
No. It shows up at the top of the Point of Care (POC) assignment screen you already work from in PCC — no separate app, no extra login.
Do I push a button to clear a flag?
No. You document in PCC. SENTINEL reads it and clears the flag on its next check.
Can this be used against me?
No. It's aggregate, never a per-person finding, and never used for personnel action.
Is SENTINEL watching for patient-safety problems?
No — that isn't its job. SENTINEL looks at the documentation, not a resident's live clinical status, so it is not a safety alarm. Patient-safety concerns are handled the way they always are — through your clinical protocols and chain of command — never through SENTINEL.
